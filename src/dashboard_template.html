Reading the result

  • Thermal fleet: . Headroom is what is left after 2025 output.
  • A drought like 2000 (hydro 42% below trend) or losing all imports is covered on its own.
  • Both together overwhelm the thermal reserve. Its availability decides how bad it gets, so move the slider.
  • Shortfall probability is conditional on imports being out. The model does not say how likely an outage is.
Lower bound on risk. No peak demand, seasonality, transmission limits or fuel logistics. Treat the numbers as the best case.

About this project

Data

  • Ember yearly electricity data: generation by source, demand, net imports, emissions. Kenya 2000 to 2025.
  • Our World in Data energy dataset: population and GDP.
  • Ten countries: Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Rwanda, Burundi, Zambia, Mozambique, DR Congo, Sudan.
  • Source totals reconcile to demand within 0.01%.

Methods

  • Five forecasting models built from scratch; rolling-origin backtests; intervals from backtest errors.
  • Security index: four pillars, winsorised and scaled 0 to 100, equal weight by default.
  • Stress tests: scenario arithmetic plus resampling of observed hydrology.
  • Capacity: gap in TWh converted to GW through capacity factors.

Limits

  • Only 26 annual points for Kenya; bands beyond three years rest on an assumption.
  • Annual energy, not peak power. No prices or costs.
  • Ember reports no geothermal capacity for Kenya, so reserve margins are not computed.
  • Thermal availability and new-plant capacity factors are assumptions.

Reproduce

Every number here is generated by the Python pipeline in the project repository. The final report and technical report explain each step and include the code.
